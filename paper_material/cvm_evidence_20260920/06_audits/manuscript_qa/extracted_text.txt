GCDL: Geometry-Constrained Distributional Localization for UA V Detection
Anonymous cvm submission
Paper ID ****
Abstract
Small objects in UA V imagery make localization sen-
sitive to boundary displacement. Fine-grained edge dis-
tributions provide a useful representation, whose in-
tegration also requires well-defined geometry and su-
pervision: signed offsets can decode to nonpositive ex-
tents, while direct IoU weighting can leave low-overlap
matches weakly supervised. We present Geometry-
Constrained Distributional Localization (GCDL), a lo-
calization design for UA V imagery that builds on D-
FINE’s representation and develops the reference path,
feasible decoding, and layerwise supervision within RT-
DETR. Geometry-feasible decoding preserves the im-
plied center and bounds each extent using reference-
scale and floating-point floors, with an explicit identity
surrogate gradient. Layerwise quality shrinkage (LQS)
redistributes detached quality weights within each im-
age and decoder layer, conserving their total while re-
ducing dispersion more strongly in early layers. A box-
conditioned expert extends this core using shared visual
features. Its localization targets are accepted only when
the proposed partial edge correction improves overlap
with the matched object. On converted-label VisDrone,
GCDL achieves 29.620 validation AP versus 29.078 for a
complete FDR/FGL transfer. The complete detector im-
proves native test AP by 5.861 points over RT-DETR-L
and common-evaluator final AP by 1.579 points over our
scratch-adapted D-FINE-L reproduction under differ-
ent training recipes. On a local converted-label UA VDT
validation split, Full improves AP from 14.12 to 16.96.
In the VisDrone configuration, Full processes 74.129 im-
ages/s at640×640, FP32, and batch eight on RTX 4090,
supporting high-throughput batch processing for UA V
imagery.
Keywords: UA V detection; distributional localization;
feasible geometry; layerwise supervision
1. Introduction
UA V aerial monitoring requires accurate localization of
small objects amid crowding and extensive background.
The scale profile in Appendix Fig. 4(a) makes this challenge
concrete: among 38,759 converted-label VisDrone [1] vali-
dation boxes, 85.9% have an equivalent side below 32 pixels
after the640×640stretch used by our common evaluator.
A fixed displacement can substantially change overlap at
these scales. For two equal-size boxes with aligned vertical
spans, a one-pixel horizontal shift reduces IoU to 0.778 at
width eight, compared with 0.939 at width 32 (Fig. 4(b)).
This scale profile motivates attention to boundary localiza-
tion.
DETR [4] formulates detection as set prediction, and RT-
DETR [5] combines efficient encoding with query-based
decoding. D-FINE [6] advances localization through fine-
grained distribution refinement (FDR), replacing point re-
gression with iteratively refined edge distributions. We
adopt this representation and investigate its decoding and
supervision within an RT-DETR host. Distributional reso-
lution describes where an edge can lie; the integration must
also define which boxes are feasible and how matched ob-
jects supervise the distributions at each depth.
These requirements lead to two design questions. Signed
edge offsets do not algebraically enforce positive decoded
extents, so a geometric constraint should preserve the im-
plied center and remain effective in finite precision. Sepa-
rately, direct matched-box IoU weighting assigns little dis-
tributional supervision to low-overlap matches. We seek
an allocation that moderates these differences in early lay-
ers while retaining quality ordering and the group’s total
weight.
We proposeGeometry-Constrained Distributional Lo-
calization(GCDL), combining an incoming-reference and
supervision-path redesign, geometry-feasible decoding,
and layerwise quality shrinkage (LQS) within RT-DETR.
Geometry-feasible decoding imposes reference-scale and
precision-aware extent floors with an identity surrogate gra-
dient. LQS shrinks eligible, detached IoU weights toward
their image–layer mean, with shrinkage decaying to zero
at the final layer. The former preserves the decoded cen-
ter and boxes whose extents already satisfy the floors; the
latter conserves total supervision weight while reducing its
dispersion. These components define the proposed local-
ization coreG, retaining RT-DETR’s classification, query
selection, and attention backbone.

Recent CVM methods further connect feature design and

complementary part of the detector: decoding and super-

Cascade-DETR [17] restricts attention using previously

samples from the existing P2–P4 pyramid, distribution
descriptors, and residual class and edge updates after base
decoding. Its local predictions serve both as the final output
and as candidate localization targets for the base layers.
2.2. Distributional localization and its integration
Deformable DETR [18] concentrates attention on sparse
locations around reference points. DN-DETR [19] and
DINO [20] improve query training through denoising.
These developments motivate efficient spatial access and
stable query optimization. GCDL focuses on the geome-
try and loss allocation of the decoded distributions within
this framework.
Generalized Focal Loss [21] represents box locations as
distributions and incorporates localization quality into de-
tection learning. This establishes a broader distributional-
regression context for the fine-grained edge representation
used here. D-FINE [6] supplies the discrete edge repre-
sentation, nonuniform support, integral decoding, cumula-
tive distribution refinement, and adjacent-bin fine-grained
localization (FGL) loss adopted here. It also studies trans-
fer to other DETR models. GCDL develops the decod-
ing and supervision of this representation within RT-DETR:
reference-scale and precision-aware extent floors impose
feasible geometry, and image–layer shrinkage moderates
quality weights while conserving their total. Figure 2(b)
details our reference, geometry, and supervision paths; Ta-
ble 3 evaluates the complete transfer and our localization
variants.
2.3. Localization knowledge transfer
Localization distillation [22] transfers distributional in-
formation from selected localization regions, while DE-
TRDistill [23] aligns teacher and student predictions
through Hungarian matching. D-FINE’s GO-LSD [6] trans-
fers final-layer localization distributions to shallower layers
using aggregated matches and quality-dependent weighting.
Our target source is a directly supervised local expert that
remains in the inference graph and supplies candidate tar-
gets to all six base layers, including the last. Selection is
defined for same-query, same-object pairs and checks the
support and relative loss of each edge. A final IoU gate
evaluates the actual partial edge replacement. This distin-
guishes the proposed selection rule from weighting a com-
plete teacher prediction by its quality alone.
3. Method
3.1. Framework and distributional representation
Our detector is organized around the proposed GCDL
localization coreG. A shared branchFrefines the hybrid
encoder’s P3 output for both the base decoder and a box-
conditioned expertB. The expert refines the final base pre-
dictions, receives direct ground-truth supervision, and sup-
plies selectively gated localization targets to the base lay-
ers. Figure 2(a) shows these prediction paths; panels (b),
(c), and (d) detailG,F, andB, respectively. The decoder
container distinguishes retained attention and classification
operations from the GCDL localization path.
Design scope.We use Ultralytics RT-DETR-L with six
decoder layers and 300 ordinary queries. The proposed
GCDL replaces its localization path while retaining the
stock classification heads, query selection, attention/FFN
backbone, matching interface, and detection losses. Its de-
sign specifies the reference and supervision path, constrains
the geometry decoded from signed distributions, and mod-
erates how matched objects supervise those distributions at
each depth. The distributional representation is inherited
from D-FINE; the incoming-reference path, feasible geom-
etry, and LQS define GCDL. Section 4.4 evaluates the com-
plete transfer and the intermediate configurations.

same geometric decoder produces the refined box. Zero-
initialized heads initially preserve the base outputs.
Direct supervision and inference.The expert reuses the
final base layer’s query-to-ground-truth assignment. Its
predictions receive classification, L1, generalized-IoU, and
fine-grained localization (FGL) losses. Direct expert losses
also update the shared feature/query path and the base log-
its through the residual connection. Sampling coordinates
and distribution descriptors are detached; teacher distribu-
tions are additionally detached when supplying localization
targets to the base decoder. At inference, the refined expert
prediction is the system output.

3.5. Training and inference
The base geometry and FGL with LQS follow Secs. 3.2
and 3.3.
LetL det contain the retained base detection losses,LFGL

For completed epocht,η(t)is zero through epoch 10, in-

ule weights the expert-to-base localization term. The ex-

active throughout training.
Inference retains the feature pyramid, shared P3 refine-
ment, distributional base decoder, local sampling, and ex-
pert heads. Matching, quality checks, and the distillation
loss are training-only operations. The architecture thus uses
local evidence to produce the final prediction and, during
training, to provide selected localization targets to the base.

5. Discussion and Conclusion
GCDL combines an incoming-reference and
supervision-path redesign with feasible decoding and
layerwise weight allocation. It builds on D-FINE’s repre-
sentation, preserves the decoded center, enforces feasible
extents, and redistributes eligible quality weights without
increasing their total. The local visual extension refines
predictions and selects transfer targets using the geometry
of the proposed partial correction.
The core exceeds the complete FDR/FGL transfer by
0.542 validation AP. Full adds 0.489 validation AP over the
core, improves native test AP over RT-DETR-L by 5.861
points, and processes 74.129 images/s at640×640, FP32,
and batch eight on RTX 4090. The additional UA VDT com-
parison records a 2.84-point AP improvement over its base-
line. These results characterize accurate UA V-image detec-
tion and high-throughput batch processing at the reported
cost.
Scope and remaining questions.The experiments in-
clude VisDrone and an additional UA VDT comparison,
while variability across training seeds remains unmeasured.
Historical localization versions do not isolate every mod-
ification, and the expert and its supervision are evaluated
jointly. The common external comparison retains different
training recipes. Broader controlled evaluation and lower-
cost expert refinement are the next priorities.

Eight nominal reference-size/input-type combinations

smallest FP16 input underflows to zero, providing an addi-

correction preserves the decoded center. These results as-
